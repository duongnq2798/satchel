Satchel (satchelnft.xyz): an NFT that owns a wallet on 8 EVM chains. Sell it, and prove nothing stayed behind.

The problem: selling a wallet with a history is easy to fake. The seller signs a withdrawal, sells the wallet, then broadcasts it. Or approves their own address to spend its tokens, then pulls them. Nothing shows the first; the second hides in each chain's logs.

What I built: a NEP-171 NFT contract on NEAR where each token controls a key NEAR's MPC network derives for it (Chain Signatures). One satchel is one address on 8 EVM mainnets and their testnets. The NFT's owner alone can have the contract sign. The contract builds every EVM transaction it signs and, before each MPC signature, writes in a public record the highest nonce signed on each chain and every approval granted; anything else is counted as opaque. A buyer's browser, or npx -y satchel-nft@0.2.0 verify, compares that record with each chain at a finalized block: every signed nonce spent, every approval revoked and no raw signature or unclassified call means provably clean. Listing freezes signing, and buy names the record version the buyer checked: one NEAR transaction moves the NFT, and the right to sign on every chain, or refunds the buyer.

The point is the check, not the NFT wallet. When a report shows an open nonce or a live approval, Make it clean spends or revokes it from the satchel's page; testnet #9 now reads provably clean.

Live on NEAR testnet and mainnet: a reproducible build, no access keys. On mainnet, satchel #1 sent ETH on Base, and #2 swapped its ETH for USDC on Arbitrum through NEAR Intents' 1Click; both read provably clean. Testnet satchels show each trick caught: a signed 10,000 ETH send kept back, an approval left live, a raw signature. Also shipped: a holder room only the owner opens (NEP-413); a dry 1Click quote; the satchel-nft library and CLI on npm; a satchel a Sputnik DAO ran by vote; a threat model; an on-chain claim checker. Not audited.
