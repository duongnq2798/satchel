Satchel (satchelnft.xyz): an NFT that owns a wallet on 8 EVM chains. Sell it, and prove nothing stayed behind.

The problem: selling a crypto wallet or an address with a history is easy to fake. The seller signs a withdrawal, sells the wallet, then broadcasts the withdrawal. Or approves their own address to spend its tokens and pulls them after the sale. A buyer can't rule either out.

What I built: a NEP-171 NFT contract on NEAR where each token controls one key NEAR's MPC network derives from the contract and the token's path (Chain Signatures). One satchel is the same address on 8 EVM mainnets and a testnet of each. The NFT's owner alone can have the contract sign. The contract builds every EVM transaction it signs and, before asking the MPC network to sign, writes in a public record the highest nonce signed on each chain and every approval granted. Anything else it signs is counted as opaque. A buyer's browser, or npx -y satchel-nft@0.2.0 verify, compares that record with each chain at a finalized block: every signed nonce spent, every approval revoked and no raw signature or unclassified call means provably clean. Listing freezes signing, and buy names the record version the buyer checked, so one NEAR transaction moves the NFT, and with it the right to sign on every chain, or refunds the buyer.

Live on NEAR testnet and mainnet, as the reproducible build of one commit with no access keys. On mainnet, satchel #1 sent ETH on Base, and #2 swapped its ETH for USDC on Arbitrum through NEAR Intents' 1Click; both read provably clean. Testnet satchels show each trick caught: a signed 10,000 ETH send kept back, an approval left live, a raw signature and refused calls. Also shipped: a holder room only the owner opens, by a signed NEP-413 message; a dry 1Click quote, with no wallet, for what a mainnet satchel holds; the satchel-nft library and CLI on npm; a satchel a Sputnik DAO ran by vote; a threat model; and a checker for the README's on-chain claims. Not audited.
