Satchel (satchelnft.xyz): an NFT that owns a wallet on 8 EVM chains. Sell it, and prove nothing stayed behind.

The problem: selling a crypto wallet, a position or an address with a history is easy to fake. The seller signs a withdrawal and keeps it, sells the wallet, then broadcasts the withdrawal and empties it. Or the seller approves an address of their own to spend the wallet's tokens and pulls them after the sale. A buyer can't rule either out.

What I built: a NEP-171 NFT contract on NEAR where each token controls one key that NEAR's MPC network derives from the contract and the token's path (Chain Signatures). One satchel is the same address on Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, OP, Polygon, BNB Chain, Avalanche and Aurora, and a testnet of each. Only the NFT's owner can have the contract sign. The contract builds every EVM transaction it signs, and before it asks the MPC network for a signature it writes down, in a public record, the highest nonce signed on each chain and every approval granted. Anything else it signs is counted as opaque. A buyer's browser, or `npx satchel-nft verify`, compares that record with each chain at a finalized block: every signed nonce spent, every approval revoked and no raw signature or call the contract could not classify means provably clean. Listing freezes signing, and `buy` names the record version the buyer checked, so one NEAR transaction moves the NFT, and with it the address on every chain, or refunds the buyer.

Live on NEAR testnet and mainnet; the report runs in the browser. On both networks the contract is the reproducible build of one commit and has no access keys. Mainnet satchel #1 has sent real ETH on Base and reads provably clean. Testnet satchels show each trick caught: a signed 10,000 ETH send kept back, an approval left live, a raw signature, and calls the contract refused. Also shipped: the satchel-nft library and CLI on npm, a threat model, and a checker for the README's on-chain claims. Not audited.
