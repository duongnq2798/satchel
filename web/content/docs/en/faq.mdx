## Basics

### What is a satchel?

An NFT on NEAR that controls one address on 16 EVM chains. Only the NFT's owner can have the
contract sign with that address's key, so whoever holds the NFT controls the address everywhere.
[How it works](/docs/how-it-works) walks through it.

### Who holds a satchel's key?

No one. NEAR's MPC network holds it in shares and signs with it only when the Satchel contract asks,
and the contract asks only for the token's current owner. The contract account has no access keys,
so no one can change that rule.

### Is the address the same on every chain?

Yes. The key is derived from the contract's account and the path `satchel/<token_id>`, so satchel
#1 has one `0x` address on all 16 chains. A testnet satchel and a mainnet satchel with the same
number have different addresses, because the contracts are different accounts.
[Satchels and addresses](/docs/concepts/satchels-and-addresses) has the derivation.

### Do I need an EVM wallet?

No. Your NEAR wallet signs the request to the contract. The app builds the EVM transaction, checks
that the contract hashes it the same way, checks the MPC signature recovers to the satchel's
address, and broadcasts it. [Send from a satchel](/docs/guides/send) shows each step.

### Which chains does it work on?

Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum One, OP Mainnet, Polygon, BNB Chain, Avalanche and Aurora, and each one's
testnet: Sepolia, Base Sepolia, Arbitrum Sepolia, OP Sepolia, Polygon Amoy, BNB testnet,
Avalanche Fuji and Aurora testnet. The list is fixed in the contract.
[Networks and chains](/docs/concepts/networks-and-chains) has the chain ids.

### Can it sign for Bitcoin or other chains?

The contract's `sign_raw` signs any 32-byte payload, such as a Bitcoin sighash. The app and the CLI
don't offer it. A raw signature is counted as opaque in the record, and that makes the satchel not
provably clean for every later owner, because no one can tell what it signed.

## Buying and selling

### What does the clean transfer report prove?

That the satchel's key left nothing behind: the contract can't sign outside the record, no signed
transaction is still waiting to be broadcast, no approval is still live, and nothing was signed that
the record can't explain. It says nothing about the assets themselves.
[The clean transfer report](/docs/concepts/clean-transfer-report) explains each check.

### Can the seller empty the satchel after I check it?

Not through the satchel's key. Listing freezes signing, and `buy` names the record version you
checked. If the seller delists and signs anything, the version goes up and your purchase fails,
with your NEAR refunded. Testnet transaction `7HQPu9qe…` shows it.
[Listings and sales](/docs/concepts/listings-and-sales) covers the rules.

### Can the previous owner still sign after a sale?

No. The contract signs only for the current owner, so the moment the NFT moves, the old owner's
requests fail with `Satchel: only the token's owner can do this`. Testnet transaction `GqFsnmFL…`
is a previous owner trying.

### Can I buy a satchel that isn't provably clean?

Yes, but you have to say so: tick the box in the app, or pass `--accept-not-clean` to
`satchel-nft buy`. Read the failing lines first. Some, like a pending nonce, the seller can fix;
the counters in check 4 never go back to 0. [Buy a satchel](/docs/guides/buy) has the details.

### How does the seller get paid?

In the same transaction as the purchase: `buy` moves the NFT to you and sends the price to the
seller. If you attached more than the price, the rest comes back to you. The contract takes no
fee. [Sell a satchel](/docs/guides/sell) is the seller's side.

### What happens if two people buy at once?

The first `buy` to run wins. The second fails with `Satchel: token is not listed`, and its deposit
is refunded.

### Does transferring a satchel end its listing?

Yes. Any transfer ends the listing, including one through `nft_transfer_call` that bounces back.
[Transfer a satchel](/docs/guides/transfer) covers transfers.

## Safety and trust

### Why should I trust the report?

You don't have to. It runs in your browser against public RPCs and names every one it read. In a
terminal, `npx satchel-nft verify <id>` does the same, and reads your own nodes if you set
`NEAR_RPC` and `RPC_<chain id>`. [Check every claim](/docs/security/check-every-claim) checks the
deployment itself.

### Has Satchel been audited?

No. It is live on mainnet, where satchels hold real funds. It has had internal reviews only (two of
the contract, one of them adversarial), and the contract's tests cover every fix. Keep in a mainnet satchel only what you can afford to
lose.

### What can't the report see?

Powers built into an asset: a token the issuer can upgrade, blacklist or claw back needs no signature
from the satchel's key. The report also trusts the RPCs it reads and the MPC network.
[Threat model](/docs/security/threat-model) lists every limit.

### Can anyone change the contract?

No. The contract account's only key was deleted before the first mint, so no one can redeploy it,
not even its author. A new version ships as a new account with a new code hash.

### What if I sign a swap or another call Satchel can't classify?

It is signed, and the record counts it as an unclassified call. That count never goes down, so the
satchel can't read as provably clean again, for you or any later owner.
[The signing record](/docs/concepts/signing-record) lists what the contract can classify.

## Fees and costs

### What does it cost?

Minting takes 0.1 NEAR, which pays for the token's storage. Each signature costs NEAR gas and
1 yoctoNEAR. A purchase costs the price plus gas. Transactions the satchel sends on an EVM chain pay
that chain's gas from the satchel's own address, in the chain's coin.

### Why does every action attach 1 yoctoNEAR?

A deposit can only come from a full-access key, so every action needs your wallet's confirmation. A
key that a website keeps for you can't sign, list or transfer a satchel.
[Wallets](/docs/guides/wallets) explains it.

## Networks

### Is Satchel on mainnet?

Yes, since 9 November 2026, at `v1.satchel-nft.near`. It is the same reproducible build as
testnet, signs through `v1.signer`, and was locked before any satchel was minted. Its app pages are
under [/mainnet](/mainnet).

### What's the difference between testnet and mainnet satchels?

They are different contracts, so they have different tokens and different addresses. The app and
the CLI send a testnet satchel's transactions only on the eight testnets, and a mainnet satchel's
only on the eight mainnets. Never send real funds to a testnet satchel's address.

## Developers

### Is there a library or an API?

The `satchel-nft` package on npm is the library and the CLI the web app runs on. It reads
satchels, runs the report and builds every transaction. [Library](/docs/developers/library) and
[CLI reference](/docs/developers/cli) cover them; the contract's methods are in the
[contract reference](/docs/developers/contract).

### How do I follow what happens on the contract?

Read its NEP-297 events: one for each signature, listing, delisting and sale, plus the NFT
standard's mint and transfer events. [Events](/docs/developers/events) has the formats and a script
that reads them.

### Can I run the app myself?

Yes. It is a Next.js app in `web/`. [Run the app](/docs/advanced/run-the-app) has the steps.
