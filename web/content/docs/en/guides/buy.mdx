Buying a satchel buys an address on 16 EVM chains and whatever it holds. The risk is what the seller could
have kept back: a signed withdrawal, an approval, a copy of the key. Satchel rules out the last by
construction and makes the others checkable, so the whole purchase comes down to reading one report and
buying at the version you read.

<Callout type="danger" title="Real funds on mainnet">
On `/mainnet` pages you pay real NEAR for an address that may hold real funds. Satchel is not audited.
</Callout>

## What you need

- A NEAR wallet connected to the satchel's network, with the price plus gas
  ([Connect a NEAR wallet](/docs/guides/wallets)). You need no EVM wallet.
- A few minutes to read the report. You can read it without a wallet, here or in a terminal.

## Buy

<Steps>

1. **Find a satchel for sale.** The [Market](/market) (`/mainnet/market` on mainnet) lists every satchel for
   sale, with its price, its seller, the record version it was listed at, and its verdict, each read in your
   browser. Click one to open its page.

2. **Read its clean transfer report.** On the satchel's page, the report runs four checks against NEAR and
   against every chain in the satchel's record, each chain at a finalized block. Read every line, not just
   the verdict:

   | Verdict | Means |
   | --- | --- |
   | <Verdict v="clean" /> | All four checks pass: nothing the satchel's key ever signed lets anyone but its owner move what it holds, as far as the RPCs it read report (see [what a clean report doesn't say](/docs/concepts/clean-transfer-report#what-a-clean-report-doesnt-say)) |
   | <Verdict v="not-provable" /> | A check failed. Its lines say which nonce, approval or counter, and why |
   | <Verdict v="unknown" /> | No check failed, but one couldn't be read: an RPC down, or a block not final yet. Click **Check again** later |

   [The clean transfer report](/docs/concepts/clean-transfer-report) explains each check.

3. **Check what's inside, and its history.** Under the address, **What's inside** shows the native balance on
   each of the network's 8 chains, and USDC, USDT and WETH where it holds some, each chain linked to its
   explorer. Any other token, a look-alike included, isn't read: look it up on the explorer. Below the report,
   **History** lists the mint, transfers, signatures, listings and sales, each linked to its NEAR transaction.
   Neither card is part of the report. The history comes from FastNEAR's transaction index, and the card warns
   when it counts a different number of signatures than the record, which is what the report checks.

4. **Click Buy for … NEAR** in the **For sale** card below the report, and approve `buy` in your wallet.
   The bar above the satchel's address shows the same price, and its **Go to buy** link scrolls down to this
   card. The call's arguments are the token id, the record version and the price; its deposit is the
   price; gas is 100 TGas. If you aren't connected, the button reads **Connect to buy**.

5. **Done.** When the purchase is final, the page reads again: **Owned by you**, and a link to the
   transaction. The address on every chain is now yours to sign for.

</Steps>

<Callout type="note" title="The demo satchels">
The README quotes the state of testnet satchels #1 to #11 and mainnet #1 to #3, so on their pages the
**For sale** card shows a note instead of the buy button until 2026-11-25. That is a guard in the app, not a
protection: the `satchel-nft` CLI and the near CLI can still buy them. To try a purchase, mint a satchel,
list it from one account and buy it from another, as [A full sale on testnet](/docs/tutorials/sale-on-testnet)
does.
</Callout>

## Why the version in your purchase matters

The purchase names the record version and the price **from the report you read**, never from the rest of
the page. The contract refuses the sale if either changed since, and you keep your NEAR:

- While a satchel is listed, its owner can't sign anything, so the record can't change.
- If the seller delists, signs, and lists again, the record version goes up, and a `buy` that names the old
  version fails with `Satchel: the signing record changed since you checked it`. On testnet,
  [`7HQPu9qe…`](https://testnet.nearblocks.io/txns/7HQPu9qeJauNrJLaMNCUuMJVska3u3uYsoGyp7gsCfWv) shows this
  for satchel #6, with the buyer's 1 NEAR refunded.
- If the seller relists at another price, the `buy` fails with `Satchel: the price changed`.

The **For sale** card guards the same way before your wallet opens:

| The card says | Why | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Waiting for the report on this listing…` | The report hasn't finished, or it read a different listing | Wait. The buy button won't proceed until the report is in |
| `The listing or its record changed while this page loaded. Check again before you buy.` | The page and the report read a different price, seller or record version (for example, the seller relisted at a new price) | Click **Check again** on the report and read it again |
| `Tick the box above to buy a satchel that is not provably clean` | The verdict isn't clean and you haven't accepted it | Read the report. Tick the box only if you accept the risk |

## Buying a satchel that isn't provably clean

You can. The card shows a checkbox:

```text
This satchel is not provably clean (see the report). I accept that the seller may still be able to move what it holds.
```

Your acceptance holds for the record version you read: if the version changes, the box asks again. Many
satchels that were used for DeFi or for swaps through a DEX router will never read clean, and that
can be fine if you price the risk. Read the failing lines: a pending nonce on a chain where the
address holds nothing is not the same risk as a live unlimited approval on the address's USDC.

## If the purchase fails

A failed `buy` changes nothing: the NFT doesn't move, NEAR refunds your deposit, and the listing stays.

| Error | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| `Satchel: the signing record changed since you checked it` | The seller signed after the version you read. Read the new report |
| `Satchel: the price changed` | The listing's price isn't the one you named |
| `Satchel: token is not listed` | It was delisted, transferred, or someone else bought it first |
| `Satchel: the seller cannot buy` | You are the seller |
| `Satchel: attach at least the price` | The deposit was below the price |

## After you buy

- **Only you can have anything new signed for the address, on every chain.** Only the token's owner can
  have the contract sign, so the seller's next `sign_evm` is refused with `Satchel: only the token's owner can do this`
  ([`GqFsnmFL…`](https://testnet.nearblocks.io/txns/GqFsnmFLcpDjJVC1b4qUPYxHRTP1Rw2vcVKxga3PVRvU) on testnet).
  What was signed before, a transaction still pending, an approval still live or a raw signature (a
  permit, say), can still move assets: that is what the report you read before buying checks. Checks 2
  and 3 look for the first two, and any raw signature fails check 4.
- **The record comes with it.** It only grows. Whatever the satchel's report read when you bought it is
  where your own report starts.
- **Any excess deposit is refunded**, and the seller is paid from your deposit.

## What the report can't see

The report proves what the satchel's key signed. It says nothing about powers built into the assets the
address holds, which need no signature from the wallet: a token the seller deployed or can upgrade, a token
with a blacklist or a clawback, an ENS name whose controller is still the seller, an ERC-4907 `user`. Value
those assets as you would anywhere else.

It is also only as honest as the RPCs it reads. The report names them under its checks. For a large
purchase, run it yourself against nodes you trust:

```bash
NEAR_RPC=https://<your-near-testnet-node> RPC_11155111=https://<your-sepolia-node> npx -y satchel-nft@0.2.0 verify 4
```

`RPC_<chain id>` works for each of the 16 chains. [RPCs, finality and rate limits](/docs/developers/rpc-and-finality)
has the details.

## In a terminal

`satchel-nft buy` runs the report first and buys only when it reads `PROVABLY CLEAN`, naming the record
version that report read:

```bash
npx -y satchel-nft@0.2.0 buy <id> --as <account>
```

Use a listed satchel that isn't a demo satchel for `<id>`, and your own account, one the near CLI can sign
for, for `<account>`.

Before anything is signed, it refuses a satchel that isn't listed, a purchase by the seller, a listing whose
version no longer matches the record, and a report that isn't clean:

```text
Not buying: the report reads NOT PROVABLE. Pass --accept-not-clean to buy it anyway.
```

Then it shows the transaction and asks before sending. For satchel #4, listed by satchel-nft.testnet for
1 NEAR, it shows:

```text
Buys satchel #4 from satchel-nft.testnet for 1 NEAR.
Names record version 0, the one this report read: if anything is signed before the sale, the contract refuses it and refunds you.
```

`--accept-not-clean` lets it buy anyway. The [CLI reference](/docs/developers/cli) has every option.
