Every satchel has a holder room at `/satchel/<id>/room` (`/mainnet/satchel/<id>/room` on mainnet). Only the
NEAR account that owns the satchel right now can open it. Sell or transfer the satchel, and the room opens for
the new holder instead: the right to open it moves with the NFT, like the right to sign for its address.

Nothing in the room is secret. It shows public chain data and steps from this documentation, so what the room
proves is the gate, not its contents.

## What the room shows

- **A holder card**: your account, the satchel and its record version, the network, the final NEAR block the
  server checked you at (linked to NearBlocks), and the key your wallet signed with.
- **A handover kit**: what to do before you sell, built from the satchel's
  [clean transfer report](/docs/concepts/clean-transfer-report), read on the server when the room opens. One
  step for each check that fails or couldn't be read, with what it means and how to fix it with the app or
  the CLI. When all four checks pass, one step: the satchel reads clean, so list it, and check again after
  any new signature.

The kit's fixes are the ones in [Sell a satchel](/docs/guides/sell#get-the-report-to-read-clean): spend each
open nonce with a send, revoke each live approval with calldata you write (the app has no revoke button),
and tell the buyer about a counter, which nothing undoes. Check 1 is about the deployment, not your
satchel, so the kit says it isn't yours to fix; while it fails, the app and the CLI sign nothing, so the
kit gives no other fix either. The app and the CLI send only on the chains of the satchel's own network:
for a chain of the other network, which a record names only if someone called `sign_evm` directly, the kit
says so instead of pointing at a button.

## Open it

<Steps>

1. **Open the room.** On a satchel's page, click **Open the holder room**, or go to `/satchel/<id>/room`.

2. **Connect the wallet** of the account that owns the satchel, on the page's network
   ([Connect a NEAR wallet](/docs/guides/wallets)).

3. **Click Open the room.** Your wallet asks you to sign a message, for `satchelnft.xyz`. It is not a
   transaction: nothing is sent to NEAR and it costs nothing. The page shows the message before you click.

4. **Read the card and the kit.** **Check again** sends the same signed proof again until the time the card
   shows, a little under 5 minutes after you signed. After that, or after a 401, it asks your wallet to
   sign a new one.

</Steps>

If you are not the holder, the room stays closed and says who owns the satchel at that block:

```text
satchel-nft.testnet owns satchel #4 at block 277042411, not you.testnet. Only its current holder can open the room.
```

## The message

The wallet signs these five lines, a NEP-413 message with recipient `satchelnft.xyz` and 32 random bytes as
its nonce (`roomMessage` in `packages/satchel-nft/src/holder.ts`):

```text
Open the holder room of Satchel #4
Network: testnet
Contract: v1.satchel-nft.testnet
Account: you.testnet
Issued: 2026-11-10T09:30:00.000Z
```

`Issued` is the time you clicked, as JavaScript's `toISOString` writes it. The page sends the signature, the
key, the nonce and that time to `/api/room`, never the message: the server writes the message again from the
satchel, the network and the account, so a signature for another satchel, network or account doesn't verify.

## How the server checks

`/api/room` is the app's one API route, and the only place its server reads NEAR: the pages read everything
in your browser. It stores nothing: no cookie, no session, no database. On every request, `checkHolder` in `packages/satchel-nft/src/holder.ts` runs these steps in order,
cheapest first, and stops at the first that fails:

| Step | Refused with |
| --- | --- |
| The body is JSON under 4 KB, every field a string, the satchel id 1 to 19 digits, the account a NEAR account of this network, the nonce 32 bytes, `Issued` a `toISOString` time | 400 (413 for a larger body) |
| `Issued` is at most 5 minutes old, and at most 1 minute ahead of the server's clock | 401 |
| The ed25519 signature verifies over the message the server wrote (`verifyNep413` in `nep413.ts`) | 401 |
| At one final NEAR block: the key is a full-access key of the account | 403 |
| At that same block: the satchel exists | 404 |
| At that same block: the account owns it | 403, naming the owner |
| The NEAR RPC answered each read within 10 seconds, about this satchel, at that block | 502 |

The RPC is read only once the signature verifies, so a request with a bad signature costs the server no read.
Once you are in, the room reads the satchel's report; if that fails or takes more than 20 seconds (an EVM
RPC that doesn't answer), you still get the holder card, with no kit and a line saying why.
Each answer to a POST is JSON with `Cache-Control: no-store`; a refusal is `{ "error": "…" }`.
Any other method gets Next.js's empty 405, with no body.

**Why a full-access key.** When you sign in to a site, your wallet may add a function-call key to your account
for that site. Such a key can sign NEP-413 messages too. Requiring a full-access key, the one your wallet signs
transactions with, keeps any site you once signed in to from opening the room as you.

## From a terminal

You can see the gate work without owning a satchel. You need [near-cli-rs](https://github.com/near/near-cli-rs)
with a NEAR testnet account in its keychain, Node 22 or later for the nonce, and bash (Git Bash on Windows).
Sign the message for satchel #4, which you don't own:

```bash
ACCOUNT=<your-account>.testnet
ISSUED=$(date -u +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%S.000Z)
# Git Bash rewrites an argument that starts with / as a path, so draw again until the nonce doesn't.
NONCE=$(node -e "let n;do{n=require('crypto').randomBytes(32).toString('base64')}while(n[0]==='/');console.log(n)")
MESSAGE="Open the holder room of Satchel #4
Network: testnet
Contract: v1.satchel-nft.testnet
Account: $ACCOUNT
Issued: $ISSUED"
near message sign-nep413 utf8 "$MESSAGE" nonce "$NONCE" recipient satchelnft.xyz sign-as "$ACCOUNT" sign-with-keychain network-config testnet
```

Use `sign-with-legacy-keychain` if your key is in `~/.near-credentials`. near-cli-rs prints JSON with a
`publicKey` and a `signature`, each already starting with `ed25519:`. Within 5 minutes of `ISSUED`, paste
them as printed and send them:

```bash
PUBLIC_KEY='<the publicKey it printed, ed25519:…>'
SIGNATURE='<the signature it printed, ed25519:…>'
curl -s -w '\n%{http_code}\n' https://satchelnft.xyz/api/room -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d "{\"network\":\"testnet\",\"tokenId\":\"4\",\"accountId\":\"$ACCOUNT\",\"publicKey\":\"$PUBLIC_KEY\",\"signature\":\"$SIGNATURE\",\"nonce\":\"$NONCE\",\"issuedAt\":\"$ISSUED\"}"
```

You should see `403` under an error naming satchel #4's owner (`satchel-nft.testnet`, until someone buys it).
Change one character of the signature and you get 401; wait 5 minutes and you get 401 again. Sign for a
satchel you own, and the same request returns the holder card and the kit as JSON.

For a mainnet satchel, the same steps take a mainnet account, `Network: mainnet` and
`Contract: v1.satchel-nft.near` in the message, `network-config mainnet` for near-cli-rs and
`"network":"mainnet"` in the body.

## Limits

- **A captured proof works until 5 minutes after its `Issued` time.** Someone who copies your signed proof
  can send it again until then, and only while your account still owns the satchel. `Issued` can be up to a
  minute ahead of the server's clock, so that is up to 6 minutes in all. The room holds nothing secret, so a
  replay shows them only public data.
- **A DAO or a multisig can't open it.** The room needs a NEP-413 signature from a key on the owning account,
  and a contract account such as a DAO or a multisig can't sign a message.
- **The server trusts the NEAR RPC it reads**: FastNEAR's public node by default, as for the report. An RPC
  that lies about the key or the owner can open the room for someone else, or keep it closed.
- **No rate limit.** The route refuses a bad signature before it reads NEAR, but it doesn't count requests.
- **Your wallet must sign messages.** Every wallet the app lists says it does; one that can't gets a message
  saying so, and the terminal steps above work instead.

The [threat model](/docs/security/threat-model#the-holder-room) lists each attack on the room and what is left.
