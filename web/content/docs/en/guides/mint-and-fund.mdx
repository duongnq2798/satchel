A satchel's address exists the moment its token does. NEAR's MPC network derives the key from the Satchel
contract's account and the token's path, `satchel/<id>`, so the address never changes, whoever owns the
token. Minting takes one NEAR transaction; funding is an ordinary transfer on an EVM chain, from any wallet
or exchange.

<Callout type="danger" title="Real funds on mainnet only">
Satchel is not audited. On mainnet, keep in a satchel only what you can afford to lose. A testnet
satchel's address is also a valid address on Ethereum and every other mainnet chain, but NEAR's testnet
MPC network signs for it: never send real funds to a testnet satchel.
</Callout>

## What you need

- A NEAR wallet connected to the network you want ([Connect a NEAR wallet](/docs/guides/wallets)), with at
  least 0.1 NEAR plus gas.
- For funding: coins on a chain of the same network. A testnet satchel sends from the 8 testnets
  (Sepolia, Base Sepolia, Arbitrum Sepolia, OP Sepolia, Polygon Amoy, BNB testnet, Avalanche Fuji,
  Aurora testnet); a mainnet satchel from the 8 mainnets (Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum One, OP Mainnet,
  Polygon, BNB Chain, Avalanche, Aurora).

## Mint a satchel

<Steps>

1. **Open Your satchels**: [satchelnft.xyz/satchels](https://satchelnft.xyz/satchels) on testnet, or
   [satchelnft.xyz/mainnet/satchels](https://satchelnft.xyz/mainnet/satchels) on mainnet, and connect your
   wallet.

2. **Wait for the contract check.** Until the network's contract passes check 1 (no access keys, the
   reproducible build, the canonical MPC contract), the page says
   `Checking the contract before showing an address…` and offers no mint button. It shows no address and
   signs nothing for a contract it hasn't checked.

3. **Click Mint a satchel · 0.1 NEAR** and approve `mint` in your wallet: 0.1 NEAR deposit, 50 TGas. The
   0.1 NEAR pays for the token's storage and its signing record; the contract refunds anything above it.

4. **Open the new satchel.** When the mint is final, the app takes you to the satchel's page,
   `/satchel/<id>` (`/mainnet/satchel/<id>` on mainnet). Your satchels also lists every token you own, each
   with its path and address.

</Steps>

The satchel's page shows its number, `Owned by you`, its path (`satchel/<id>`) and its record version
(0 for a new satchel), then a card headed **One address on 16 EVM chains, controlled by whoever owns this

## Fund it

<Steps>

1. **Copy the address** from the satchel's page. It is the same `0x` address on all 16 chains.

2. **Send coins to it on one chain of the right network**, from any wallet or exchange that sends on that
   chain. A testnet satchel: Sepolia ETH, Base Sepolia ETH, Polygon Amoy POL and so on. A mainnet satchel:
   real ETH, POL, BNB or AVAX.

3. **Check what's inside.** Under the address, **What's inside** shows the native balance on each of the
   network's 8 chains, and USDC, USDT and WETH where the satchel holds some. Each chain links to the address
   on its explorer, which also shows any other token, and a balance reads `unavailable` when the chain's RPC
   doesn't answer. Click **Check again** on the report to read everything again.

</Steps>

From a terminal, `show` prints the native balances (built from this repository, its USDC, USDT and
WETH too). This is satchel #4 on testnet:

```bash
npx -y satchel-nft@0.1.0 show 4
```

explorer shows them, but the app doesn't list them yet.

## Mint first, then fund

A satchel's address is fixed by its id before the token exists, so you can compute the next satchel's
address in advance. Don't fund it in advance. `mint` is open to anyone, and the next id goes to whoever
mints first: if someone else mints it between your funding and your mint, they own the address and what
you sent to it.

This is not hypothetical. On mainnet, satchel #1's address was funded with 0.00195 ETH on Base 92 seconds
before it was minted. Its owner minted it in time; anyone who had minted #1 in between would have owned
that ETH. Always fund the address your wallet shows on a satchel you already own.

## Fees on a new address

Your satchel pays for its own EVM transactions from its own balance, so fund it with enough for the value
you plan to send plus gas. When the app sends from a satchel, it estimates gas for that exact transaction
and adds 20%, and sets the fee cap at twice the latest base fee plus the priority fee, since the MPC
signature takes a few NEAR blocks to arrive. Before your wallet opens, it checks that the balance covers
the value plus the most the fees can reach, including the L1 data fee on OP Mainnet, Base and their Sepolia
testnets. [Send from a satchel](/docs/guides/send#the-checks-before-anything-is-signed) has the details.

Sending to an address that has never been used can cost far more gas than a plain 21,000-gas transfer. When
the demo satchels were funded on Sepolia, each send to a new address used 204,600 gas. The app estimates
gas, so you never set it by hand, but fund enough for it.

## Common mistakes

- **Funding before minting.** See [above](#mint-first-then-fund).
- **Funding a testnet satchel with real coins.** The address is valid on mainnet chains, but only the
  testnet MPC network signs for it. The app never sends mainnet coins from a testnet satchel.
- **Funding the wrong network's chain.** A mainnet satchel's app sends only on the 8 mainnets. Coins on a
  testnet sit at the same address, but you can't send them from `/mainnet` pages.
- **Expecting a transfer to reset the address.** It doesn't: the address and the
  [signing record](/docs/concepts/signing-record) stay with the token for life.
