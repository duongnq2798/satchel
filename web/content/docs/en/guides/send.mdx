Sending from a satchel is two transactions. Your NEAR wallet approves one call, `sign_evm`, to the Satchel
contract. The contract builds and hashes the EVM transaction, writes its nonce into the satchel's public
[signing record](/docs/concepts/signing-record), and asks NEAR's MPC network to sign it. The app then
broadcasts the signed transaction on the EVM chain. You need no EVM wallet, and the satchel pays the gas.

<Callout type="danger" title="Mainnet sends move real funds">
On `/mainnet` pages, a send moves real coins and can't be undone. Satchel is not audited: keep in a
satchel only what you can afford to lose.
</Callout>

## What you need

- A satchel you own, **not listed**. The send panel appears only on the page of a satchel you own that is
  not for sale: a listed satchel can't sign. [Delist it](/docs/guides/sell#delist) first if it is.
- A balance on the chain you send from, enough for the value plus the most the fees can reach.
  [Fund it](/docs/guides/mint-and-fund) first.
- Your NEAR wallet, connected to the same network ([Connect a NEAR wallet](/docs/guides/wallets)).
- A satchel that isn't a demo satchel. The README quotes the state of testnet #1 to #8 and mainnet #1 and
  #2, so on their pages the panel shows a note instead of the form until 2026-11-25. That is a guard in the
  app, not a protection: the `satchel-nft` CLI and the near CLI can still sign for them.
  [Mint your own](/docs/guides/mint-and-fund) to try this.

## Send

<Steps>

1. **Open your satchel's page** (`/satchel/<id>`, or `/mainnet/satchel/<id>`) and find **Send from this
   satchel**.

2. **Fill in the form.**

   | Field | What to enter |
   | --- | --- |
   | Chain | One of the network's 8 chains. Testnet pages list only testnets; `/mainnet` pages only mainnets |
   | To | The recipient's `0x` address. Checksummed, if it mixes upper and lower case |
   | Amount | In the chain's coin: ETH, POL, BNB, tBNB or AVAX. Empty means 0 |
   | Calldata (advanced) | `0x` for a plain transfer, the default. To call a contract, its ABI-encoded calldata |

3. **Click Sign and send.** The panel lists the steps as they run, and marks each one done (✓), running
   (…) or failed (✗):

   ```text
   Build the transaction and check the balance
   Check the contract hashes it the same way
   Approve sign_evm in your wallet; NEAR's MPC network signs
   Check the signature recovers to this satchel's address
   Broadcast
   Wait for the receipt
   ```

4. **Approve `sign_evm` in your wallet** when it opens: 1 yoctoNEAR deposit, 300 TGas. While the wallet asks,
   the panel shows the most the satchel can pay, as `(fees up to 0.000… ETH, paid by the satchel)`. Read
   the wallet's prompt: its arguments are the EVM transaction the contract will sign.

5. **Wait for the result.** `Sent.` when the transaction succeeded, or `Included, but the transaction
   reverted.`, shown as a warning, when it was mined and failed (a reverted transaction still spends its
   nonce and its gas). Screen readers hear which step is running, then the error or the result.
   The panel links to the transaction on the chain's explorer (**View on Sepolia**) and to the call on
   NEAR (**sign_evm on NEAR**). The links stay up even if a later step fails.

</Steps>

## The checks before anything is signed

Each check runs before the step that would spend anything. The same code, `sendFromSatchel` in the
`satchel-nft` package, runs in the app and in the CLI.

| Step | What is checked | If it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Build | The chain is one of this network's 8. The chain's RPC answers with that chain's id. The balance covers the value; then, with gas estimated, it covers the value plus gas at the fee cap plus, on OP Mainnet, Base, OP Sepolia and Base Sepolia, twice the current L1 data fee | Nothing is signed |
| Hash | The contract's `evm_signing_hash` for the transaction equals the hash viem computes in your browser | `The contract would sign 0x…, not 0x…. Nothing was signed.` |
| Wallet | Your wallet calls `sign_evm`. The contract checks that you own the satchel, that it isn't listed, and that the chain is supported, then records the nonce and asks the MPC network | The contract refuses the call; see [errors](/docs/developers/errors) |
| Recover | The MPC signature recovers to the satchel's own address | `The signature recovers to 0x…, not this satchel's 0x…. Nothing was broadcast.` |
| Broadcast | The app sends the signed transaction to the chain's RPC | The error is shown, with the links |
| Confirm | The app waits up to 3 minutes for the receipt | The error is shown; check the explorer link |

How the transaction is built:

- **Nonce:** the address's next nonce, counting pending transactions.
- **Gas:** estimated for this exact transaction, plus 20% (21,000 when the estimate is 21,000 or less). A
  send to a contract runs its code and needs more than 21,000; a send to an address that has never been
  used can too. When the demo satchels were funded on Sepolia, each such send used 204,600 gas.
- **Fees:** the priority fee the chain suggests, and a fee cap of twice the latest base fee plus that
  priority fee. The headroom is there because the MPC signature takes a few NEAR blocks to arrive.

## What a send does to the record

`sign_evm` writes to the satchel's record **before** the MPC network signs, in the same receipt, and nothing
that happens to the signature afterwards can undo it:

- the chain's highest nonce signed (`max_nonce`) and its signature count;
- the call's class: a value transfer (empty calldata), an asset transfer, an approval (itemized, up to 32),
  a revocation (logged, not itemized), or unclassified (counted);
- the record version, which goes up by one.

So a send always leaves a trace that a buyer will check. A plain transfer of coins is harmless to the
report once its nonce is spent and final. Calldata is where you can hurt your satchel's report for good.
The panel warns you under **Calldata (advanced)**:

```text
The contract classifies the call. Approvals are itemized and must be revoked before a clean sale; anything
it can't classify makes this satchel unprovable for every later owner.
```

[The signing record](/docs/concepts/signing-record) lists exactly which calls are classified how.

## After you send: the pending window

The MPC signature is public in the NEAR receipt of your `sign_evm` call. Until a transaction at that nonce
is in a **finalized** block on the chain, anyone could broadcast the signed transaction. So right after a
send, the satchel's report reads <Verdict v="unknown" /> for that chain:

```text
Sepolia: signed up to nonce 4; a transaction at that nonce is in a recent block that is not finalized yet. Check again once it is
```

Wait for the chain to finalize the block, then click **Check again**. On Sepolia this takes about 13
minutes; when satchel #1 sent on Base mainnet, its report read clean 21 minutes after the send.

## If something goes wrong

| You see | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `This satchel holds … wei on Sepolia and the transaction can cost up to …. Fund 0x… first.` | The balance doesn't cover the value plus the most the fees can reach | Fund the address, or send less. Nothing was signed |
| `Sepolia says this transaction would fail, so nothing was signed: …` | Gas estimation failed: the call would revert, or the address can't pay | Check the recipient, amount and calldata |
| `Couldn't read Sepolia's priority fee, so nothing was signed: …` | The chain's RPC didn't answer | Try again later |
| `Enter the recipient's 0x address` / `Enter an amount in ETH` / `Calldata must be 0x followed by whole bytes` | The form is invalid | Fix the field |
| `sign_evm (…) finished without a signature. Your record shows the nonce; signing again is safe.` | The contract recorded the nonce, but the transaction returned no signature. If the MPC request itself fails, you see its failure message instead | Send again. The record already counts that nonce, and the next send uses it |
| An error after **Broadcast** | The transaction may still have reached the chain | Open the explorer link **before** you send again, or you may pay twice |
| `Satchel: signing is frozen while the token is listed` | The satchel is listed | Delist it first |
| `Satchel: only the token's owner can do this` | The connected account doesn't own the satchel | Connect the owner's account |

A signed transaction you never broadcast keeps the satchel unprovable until its nonce is spent. Sending
again from the app spends it: the app uses the next unspent nonce, which is that one. **Make it clean**,
the card under the report, counts the open nonces on each chain and offers a **Spend nonce** button for
each one a revoke there doesn't spend: see [Spend every signed nonce](/docs/guides/sell#spend-every-signed-nonce).

## In a terminal

`satchel-nft send` runs the same flow and checks, with the near CLI signing `sign_evm` from its keychain:

```bash
npx -y satchel-nft@0.2.0 send <id> --chain sepolia --to 0x000000000000000000000000000000000000dEaD --value 0.0001 --as <account>
```

Use a satchel you minted for `<id>`, and its owner, an account the near CLI can sign for, for `<account>`.
`--chain` takes a chain id or its name (`8453`, `base`, `"Base Sepolia"`); `--data` takes calldata. Before
you type `yes`, it shows the value, the recipient, the fee cap and the nonce the contract will record. If the
broadcast or its receipt fails, it exits 3 with the transaction's hash and its nonce: check that hash before
you send again. The [CLI reference](/docs/developers/cli) has every option.
